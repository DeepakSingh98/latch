Open problems

This preview includes runnable examples for a limited set of models and learning rules.

Performance

  1. Idle time at grid barriers. Each interval ends at a grid barrier, so every SM waits for the slowest one. A per-SM trace of one TinyMoE update on an H100 shows SMs waiting 13% of the time at 32 × 1024 and 20% at 2 × 1024. At 32 × 1024, each layer’s expert-dispatch gather runs on one SM and its bucket ordering on 16 while the other SMs wait, about 2 ms per update. Starting each task when its own inputs are ready, instead of at the next grid barrier, could recover part of this time.
  2. Fewer barrier-separated phases. Granite spends about 25 ms on 1,200 small elementwise, normalization and routing intervals. Standalone elementwise phases take 55% of E2-TTT’s memory-region time at four sequences. Combining these operations could reduce that cost.
  3. Large-batch efficiency. The current compiler’s same-session update speedup is about 2.75× at 2 × 1024 and 1.67× at 32 × 1024 against the conventional trunk-graph control. An older measurement saved about 10% at the published batch of 16 × 8 × 1024. Granite remains slower at 4,096 tokens.
  4. Long-sequence attention. On Granite at 4,096 tokens, Latch's attention takes about 20 ms per update against about 14 ms for FlashAttention-2.
  5. Gradients of repeated gathers. Top-8 routing sends each token to eight experts. Latch’s deterministic backward scatter takes about 32 ms against about 5 ms conventionally. The number of contributions is known in advance. A direct sum per row may be faster.
  6. Feed-forward blocks. Latch’s feed-forward blocks remain 1.24–1.45× slower than compiled PyTorch.

Generality

  1. New models and learning algorithms. Port another public model or test a learning rule that uses frequent small updates.
  2. Smaller activation arenas. The current compiler includes opt-in lifetime and recomputation rules (memory_rules). E2-TTT’s B4 remat_absorbed program matches the validated memory-lane program, which reduced peak allocated memory from 75.3 to about 50 GiB. Broader automatic planning remains open; the published B8 setting was estimated at 137 GiB before recomputation. E2-TTT’s research runner is not included.
  3. Multiple GPUs. This release runs on one H100. The experimental distributed-training implementation is excluded. Earlier two-GPU research was slower than one GPU on TinyMoE and missed a late-training numerical check. A convincing future test needs a model too large for one GPU.
  4. Other GPUs. Latch generates programs for NVIDIA H100 only.

Correctness

  1. Keep numerical checks broad. The current compiler fixes the rpackb gated-attention defect by tracking arena lifetimes from the earliest writer. The gated TinyMoE checks pass. This fix does not requalify the older skips-and-gates learning result, whose separate trained-state audit remains failed.
  2. Numerical checks for BF16 expert models. Tested BF16 implementations of Granite differ from FP64 by a median 25–33% on individual accumulation microbatches at trained states. Latch and PyTorch also sometimes exceed the 0.08 gradient gate at TinyMoE’s trained 2 × 512 states. The latest tuned 2 × 1024 and recipe-Q candidate states pass their tested gates; these are different programs. Keep the unchanged gates and the failures visible when testing new candidates.

Build

  1. Faster lowering. Earlier 0.9.0rc1 measurements cut TinyMoE build time from 544 to 283 s on 64 vCPUs with identical output. Lowering still uses single-threaded Python and takes the largest share of most builds.
  2. Automatic build configuration. The compiler could choose device units, outlining and shared-memory reserves. These require explicit flags today.
